My research focuses on statistical methodology, optimal experimental design, computational statistics, optimization, and machine learning. I am particularly interested in developing computational methods for complex statistical problems, including multi-objective design optimization and robust statistical modeling.

01

Optimal Experimental Design

My research in optimal experimental design focuses on constructing efficient designs for statistical modeling and scientific experimentation. It considers multiple design objectives, computational optimization, and the trade-offs that arise when different statistical criteria must be satisfied simultaneously. This work aims to make optimal design methods more flexible and computationally practical for complex experimental settings.

Multi-Objective Optimal Experimental Design

This work develops particle-swarm-based optimization methods for constructing Pareto-optimal experimental designs under multiple competing criteria, including I-, G-, and D-optimality. The goal is to efficiently characterize trade-offs among different design objectives and provide a collection of candidate designs rather than a single optimal solution.

Input-Response Experimental Design

Yang, X., Lu, L., & Anderson-Cook, C. M. (2024). Input-response space-filling designs incorporating response uncertainty. Statistical Analysis and Data Mining: The ASA Data Science Journal, 17(1), e11648. DOI

Develops an input-response space-filling design framework that incorporates response uncertainty, allowing the design to account for coverage in both the experimental input space and the resulting response space.

02

Sequential Experimental Design

My research in sequential experimental design focuses on efficient data collection when experiments can be conducted and updated over multiple stages. It includes space-filling design, adaptive selection of new experimental points, and design augmentation based on information already collected. These methods are particularly useful for exploring complex design spaces while controlling experimental cost and improving the information obtained from each stage.

Space-Filling Design Methodology

Yang, X., Lu, L., & Anderson-Cook, C. M. (2026). Quick Input-Response Space-Filling (QIRSF) Designs. Quality and Reliability Engineering International. DOI

Develops a fast and scalable input-response space-filling approach that jointly considers input-space coverage and anticipated response behavior while substantially reducing computational cost.

Develops a scalable method for non-uniform space-filling designs that allows experimental points to be concentrated in regions requiring greater attention.

Yang, X., Lu, L., & Anderson-Cook, C. M. (2024). Input-response space-filling designs incorporating response uncertainty. Statistical Analysis and Data Mining: The ASA Data Science Journal, 17(1), e11648. DOI

Extends input-response space-filling design by incorporating uncertainty in anticipated responses into the design construction process.

03

Statistical Methodology & Applications

My research in statistical methodology connects statistical modeling with real-world data analysis. It includes regression and longitudinal methods, statistical inference, and data-driven approaches for understanding complex relationships in observational data. Applications have covered transportation, mobility, travel behavior, equity, resilience, and other social and behavioral problems, demonstrating how statistical methods can support both scientific understanding and practical decision-making.

04

Computational Statistics & Machine Learning

My research in computational statistics and machine learning combines statistical methodology with modern computational tools for analyzing complex data. It includes robust statistical modeling, optimization, interpretable machine learning, survival analysis, and neural-network methods. A central interest is understanding not only predictive performance, but also model robustness and interpretation, with applications ranging from behavioral and transportation data to biomedical and health-related classification problems.

Robust Logistic Modeling and Neural Networks

This work compares conventional likelihood and cross-entropy approaches with alternative distance-based estimation methods. The methods are evaluated through simulation studies and real-world classification problems, including biomedical and health-related data, with attention to robustness, predictive performance, and computational implementation.
